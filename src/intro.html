What is Sweep?

Sweep — Software Engineering Essential Practices — is a curated map of engineering concerns, practices, and tools. It connects discovery, delivery, and operations so teams can ask better questions, find useful references, and learn from production. It is a discussion aid; it does not assess a team’s maturity or certify compliance.

Why I made it

Over the years I have watched teams rediscover the same practices one hard lesson at a time: discovery skipped until the wrong thing ships, testing bolted on late, architecture decisions nobody wrote down. The knowledge exists — it is just scattered across books, talks, and blog posts, with little guidance on what matters first.

Sweep puts those connections in one picture. The notes explain purpose and trade-offs, and link to definitions, research, and example techniques. The selection and tags are editorial judgments; a reference does not validate every recommendation in a note.

How to read it

Sweep 1.2 uses two reading cues: Review routinely means revisit the concern and decide what adequate coverage looks like. Context dependent means choose the practice when a concrete need justifies its cost and prerequisites. Neither tag measures execution quality or sets an adoption order.

Choose one improvement

Start with a user problem, risk, or delivery constraint. Pick a relevant practice, record why it applies, assign an owner, and define observable evidence and a review date. Include the setup and maintenance cost. Compare the result with a baseline and adapt or stop when it does not help. If a practice does not apply, record the reason and when to revisit it.

The chart combines capabilities, methods, tools, and team conditions. Checkmarks are personal tracking aids stored in this browser; a completion percentage is not an engineering score. Keep evidence and team decisions in your normal shared records.
