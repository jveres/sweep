What is Sweep?

Sweep — Software Engineering Essential Practices — is a curated map of the practices that keep engineering clean: a practical path to better quality at higher speed. It is organized as a roadmap rather than a checklist, because the practices build on each other — it all starts with learning and critical thinking.

Why I made it

Over the years I have watched teams rediscover the same practices one hard lesson at a time: discovery skipped until the wrong thing ships, testing bolted on late, architecture decisions nobody wrote down. The knowledge exists — it is just scattered across books, talks, and blog posts, with little guidance on what matters first.

Sweep is my attempt to put that path in one picture: opinionated enough to give a starting order, honest enough to link the sources instead of paraphrasing them. Every topic points to the material I would actually hand a colleague.

How to read it

Sweep 1.1 groups practices by maturity: start with the foundation tier, adopt the contextual practices when they apply to your situation, and add the advanced ones at scale. Sweep 1.0 is the original edition, kept for reference.

The chart is interactive: click a topic for its note and links, tick off what your team already does, and your progress stays in this browser. The settings menu switches theme and appearance; the selector above swaps between editions.

Use these definitions as a shared vocabulary, and adapt each practice to the product's risk, scale, and operating context.
